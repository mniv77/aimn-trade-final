1. The Core Principle — KISS

AiMn is intentionally moving away from the idea that a large collection of traditional indicators is required to trade successfully. The core philosophy is KISS — Keep It Simple.

The primary question is simple: Has the market changed trend? We do not need to predict every movement. We need to recognize meaningful transitions as close as possible to where they occur.

2. Three Market States

LONG

The market is generally moving upward.

SHORT

The market is generally moving downward.

FLAT

The market is moving sideways without a clear direction.

3. Entry Is a Trend Transition

The strategy does not enter simply because an indicator reaches a particular number. An entry occurs when the market transitions into a tradable direction.

SHORT → LONG = ENTER LONG
LONG → SHORT = ENTER SHORT
FLAT → LONG = ENTER LONG
FLAT → SHORT = ENTER SHORT
LONG → FLAT = normally no new trade
SHORT → FLAT = normally no new trade

FLAT is a waiting state. A transition from FLAT into LONG or SHORT can create an entry. A transition from LONG or SHORT into FLAT is not, by itself, a reason to open a trade.

4. The Ideal Reversal — V-Long and V-Short

The ideal trend reversal can look like a V. A V with the low point at the bottom represents a reversal toward LONG — V-Long. An upside-down V represents a reversal toward SHORT — V-Short.

V
V-Long

Downtrend changes to uptrend.

∧
V-Short

Uptrend changes to downtrend.

Real markets are rarely this perfect. Reversals can look like M, W, U, inverted U, rounded turns, or irregular formations. The shape itself is not the strategy. The change from one trend state to another is the strategy.

5. Detecting the Transition Efficiently

A scanner may examine an asset only every few minutes. We do not want it to repeatedly travel far back through the entire candle history to rediscover the same trend.

Once a trend has been established, the system remembers its state and the relevant transition/candle time. When the scanner returns, it can concentrate on the candles that appeared since the previous inspection.

Conceptual flow

  1. Determine the latest established trend.
  2. Remember the trend and its relevant candle/time.
  3. Return later and inspect the new candles.
  4. If the new candles continue the same trend, update the remembered point.
  5. If a new trend develops and survives confirmation, recognize a genuine transition.

6. Minor Noise vs. Major Trend Change

While riding a LONG trend, the market can briefly turn toward SHORT and then return to LONG. The same can happen while riding SHORT. These local reversals are noise unless they become persistent enough to represent a real transition.

Example while riding LONG

LONG → possible SHORT → LONG

If the change does not survive the confirmation zone, treat it as minor noise and continue riding the LONG trend.

For a suspected transition, the system should allow approximately 2–4 candles for confirmation. The purpose is to avoid exiting a good trade because of a small local fluctuation.

Major noise / confirmed reversal

LONG → SHORT → SHORT → SHORT

If the transition survives the safety zone, it may be a genuine trend change. The LONG position can be exited, and the system can wait for the next appropriate transition before re-entering.

7. Exiting a Trade

The normal exit follows the same fundamental idea as the entry: the trade should end when the trend changes.

A trailing take-profit is useful because it follows the favorable movement of the trade and can identify a meaningful retracement from the established peak. It should not be treated as a simple profit target that exits every time the market makes a small pullback.

LONG

Ride the upward trend. A confirmed reversal toward SHORT can end the LONG trade.

SHORT

Ride the downward trend. A confirmed reversal toward LONG can end the SHORT trade.

8. Unexpected Market Shock — RSI as Emergency Protection

Trend-change detection needs time to establish that a reversal is real. Markets can occasionally move so violently that waiting for normal confirmation would be dangerous.

This is where RSI has a very different role. RSI is not the engine of the strategy. It is an emergency protection mechanism for extraordinary situations.

LONG + sudden catastrophic drop: RSI can trigger an emergency exit before normal trend confirmation catches up.
SHORT + sudden explosive rise: RSI can trigger an emergency exit before normal trend confirmation catches up.

If the unexpected movement happens in our favor, the normal trend/trailing logic can continue to manage the profitable trade. The emergency RSI protection exists for the dangerous opposite case.

9. Stop Loss — Final Protection

A stop loss provides another layer of protection when the strategy is wrong or the market behaves in a way the normal trend logic cannot handle. It is a safety mechanism, not the reason for entering a trade.

1. Trend transition — primary trading decision.
2. Trailing take-profit — normal trade management and reversal protection.
3. RSI emergency protection — fast response to extreme unexpected moves.
4. Stop loss — final protection when the trade goes seriously wrong.

10. One Strategy for Real-Time Trading and Backtesting

The real-time scanner and the backtest system should use the same strategy logic. There should not be one set of rules for paper/live trading and another set for backtesting.

Market candles
↓
Shared trend / transition decision
↓
Real-time paper/live execution
Backtest / auto-tuner

This prevents the dangerous situation where a backtest says one thing while the real scanner follows different entry and exit rules.

11. The Future — AiMn AI Vision

The long-term objective is for AiMn to learn to view a live chart in a way similar to an experienced human trader: recognize the current trend, recognize when a transition is developing, distinguish normal noise from a meaningful reversal, and identify the best practical entry or exit point.

The future AI should learn from many different reversal shapes — V, inverted V, M, W, U, rounded and irregular formations — rather than being taught that one particular shape is required.

Eventually, AI Vision may also help determine whether an apparent reversal is temporary noise worth riding out or a major change where exiting and waiting for the next global trend transition is wiser.

12. The Strategy in One Sentence

Follow the trend, enter as close as possible to a real trend transition, ignore minor noise, exit when a meaningful transition occurs, and use RSI and stop loss only to protect against the unexpected.
