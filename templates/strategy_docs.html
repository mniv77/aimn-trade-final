PART 1

The Simple Explanation

For anyone who wants to understand the idea without knowing anything about trading software.

KISS — Keep It Simple

AiMn is intentionally moving away from the idea that a large collection of traditional indicators is required to trade successfully. The main question is very simple:

“Has the market changed direction?”

We are not trying to predict every little movement. We want to recognize a real change of trend as close as reasonably possible to where it happens.

Three basic market conditions

LONG

The market is generally going up.

SHORT

The market is generally going down.

FLAT

The market is moving sideways without a clear direction.

When do we enter?

SHORT → LONG = ENTER LONG
LONG → SHORT = ENTER SHORT
FLAT → LONG = ENTER LONG
FLAT → SHORT = ENTER SHORT
LONG → FLAT = normally do not enter
SHORT → FLAT = normally do not enter

FLAT is mainly a waiting condition. We care about a change from FLAT into a clear LONG or SHORT direction. A change from LONG or SHORT into FLAT is not, by itself, a reason to open a trade.

The ideal picture: V-Long and V-Short

The perfect reversal can look like a V. When the bottom of the V marks a change from falling to rising, we call it a V-Long. An upside-down V represents a change from rising to falling, which we call a V-Short.

V
V-Long

Falling → Rising

∧
V-Short

Rising → Falling

Real charts are rarely perfect. The reversal may look like an M, W, U, an upside-down U, a rounded turn, or something much more complicated. The shape is not the strategy. The transition between trends is the strategy.

Noise

Markets do not move perfectly. A LONG trend can briefly turn toward SHORT and then immediately return to LONG. That small movement is noise. We do not want every little wiggle to make us buy, sell, exit, and re-enter.

LONG → possible SHORT → LONG

If the change does not survive confirmation, we treat it as minor noise and continue the trade.

A more important reversal that remains in the new direction can become a genuine trend change. The normal transition is allowed approximately 2–4 candles for confirmation so that small noise does not control the trade.

PART 2

How AiMn Sees the Change

The same idea, now explained as if we are teaching the computer how to watch the chart.

Think of AiMn as a person watching a chart

Imagine a person looking at a chart every few minutes. They do not need to redraw the entire history every time they look. They remember what they were seeing last time and concentrate on what happened since then.

AiMn should work the same way.

The old way: keep searching backward

One possible method is to go backward through many candles, then move forward again until the computer finds where one trend ended and another began. That can work, but it repeats work that has already been done.

The AiMn memory method

Instead, when AiMn discovers the latest established trend, it remembers two simple things:

WHAT?

The latest known state: LONG, SHORT, or FLAT.

WHEN?

The candle/time associated with that known state or transition.

Simple example

First visit: AiMn determines that BTC is LONG.
Memory: Trend = LONG and Time = the latest established candle.
Next visit: three new candles have appeared.
AiMn: checks those new candles instead of searching the whole history again.
If they remain LONG: move the remembered point forward.
If they become SHORT and stay SHORT: a new transition has been confirmed.
OLD MEMORY: LONG @ 10:30
↓ new candles arrive ↓
NEW MEMORY: LONG @ 10:45
or, if a confirmed reversal occurs:
NEW TRANSITION: SHORT

Minor noise versus a real transition

A suspected reversal is not automatically accepted. AiMn gives the new direction a small confirmation window—approximately 2–4 candles—to determine whether the movement is continuing or was only a temporary wiggle.

MINOR NOISE

LONG → SHORT → LONG

New direction fails confirmation. Continue the existing trend.

MEANINGFUL CHANGE

LONG → SHORT → SHORT → SHORT

New direction survives confirmation. Treat it as a real transition.

The four important transition types

SHORT → LONG = LONG opportunity
LONG → SHORT = SHORT opportunity
FLAT → LONG = LONG opportunity
FLAT → SHORT = SHORT opportunity

LONG → FLAT and SHORT → FLAT are not automatically entries. They mainly tell the system that the market has lost its clear direction.

Exiting is also a trend-change decision

When riding LONG, we want to stay with the trend rather than panic over every small pullback. A trailing take-profit can follow the profitable movement and help identify a meaningful reversal. When riding SHORT, the same principle works in the opposite direction.

If a reversal is only minor noise, we continue. If the transition passes the confirmation zone and becomes meaningful, we exit the existing trade and wait for the next appropriate transition rather than immediately guessing.

PART 3

Technical Implementation

The same strategy expressed clearly enough for the software, backtester, tuner, and future AI to follow.

1. One strategy — not several strategies

The real-time scanner, paper/live trading system, backtester, and auto-tuner should use the same core trend-transition strategy. We should not create separate versions of the entry and exit logic for each application.

CANDLE DATA
↓
SHARED TREND / TRANSITION ENGINE
↓
Real-Time Scanner
Backtester
Auto-Tuner

2. One brain, separate memories

Sharing the strategy does not mean that every application should share the same state record. Each application or independent run needs its own memory so that it cannot accidentally change the state being used by another application.

SAME RULES
Live Trader

Own state/memory for its current market process.

Backtest

Own state/memory for the historical run.

Auto-Tuner

Own state/memory for each independent test/run.

Think of it as one teacher and several students. The teacher gives everyone the same rules. Each student keeps their own notebook.

3. Entry rules

  1. Determine the current market state: LONG, SHORT, or FLAT.
  2. Compare it with the previously remembered state.
  3. Detect a transition into LONG or SHORT.
  4. Allow the transition to pass the confirmation window, approximately 2–4 candles.
  5. Ignore a short-lived reversal that returns to the previous trend.
  6. Enter as close as practical to the confirmed transition rather than entering late after a large part of the move has already happened.

4. Exit rules

  1. While LONG, follow the upward trend.
  2. While SHORT, follow the downward trend.
  3. Use the trailing take-profit to follow favorable movement and detect a meaningful retracement/reversal.
  4. Do not exit merely because of minor local noise.
  5. If a meaningful transition is confirmed, exit the current trade.
  6. After an exit, wait for the next appropriate transition rather than guessing an immediate re-entry.

5. Unexpected market shock — RSI emergency protection

Normal trend-change detection needs confirmation. But sometimes the market moves like a bomb has gone off: terrible news can cause a LONG position to collapse suddenly, or extraordinary good news can cause a SHORT position to explode upward.

In those situations, waiting for several candles may be too slow. RSI therefore has a very specific secondary job:

LONG + sudden extreme drop

RSI emergency protection may force an early exit before normal trend confirmation finishes.

SHORT + sudden extreme rise

RSI emergency protection may force an early exit before normal trend confirmation finishes.

RSI is not the strategy. It is emergency protection against the unexpected.

6. Stop loss — final safety net

The stop loss is another protection layer for a trade that goes seriously wrong. It is not the reason for entering the trade.

Primary: trend transition.
Normal trade management: trailing take-profit and meaningful reversal detection.
Emergency: RSI protection against extreme unexpected movement.
Final protection: stop loss.

7. Future AiMn AI Vision

The long-term goal is for AiMn to learn to look at a live chart in a way that resembles an experienced human observer: recognize LONG, SHORT, and FLAT; recognize when a transition is developing; distinguish minor noise from a meaningful reversal; and identify an entry or exit close to the important transition.

The AI should not be limited to recognizing a V. It should learn from V-Long, V-Short, M, W, U, rounded turns, and irregular formations. The common feature is the change of trend, not the shape.

Eventually, AI can also help decide whether a developing reversal is only noise worth riding through or a major change where exiting and waiting for the next global trend is wiser.

8. The strategy in one paragraph

AiMn keeps the trading idea simple: identify whether the market is LONG, SHORT, or FLAT; enter when a meaningful transition creates a new tradable direction; confirm enough to avoid minor noise; stay with the trend while it remains valid; exit when a meaningful transition occurs; use RSI for sudden dangerous shocks and stop loss as final protection; and make the same core strategy available to real-time trading, backtesting, tuning, and eventually AI Vision.

The most important rule

ONE STRATEGY. ONE SET OF RULES. DIFFERENT APPLICATIONS, DIFFERENT MEMORIES.

The implementation should follow this document rather than reinventing the trading logic in each part of the system.
